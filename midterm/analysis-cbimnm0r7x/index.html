Midterm 19.11: lecturer report

In short

  • Calibration: on average a 12-point gap between the share who called a question hard and the share who got it wrong (11 weighted by points). Students saw the exam as somewhat easier than their results (-10 on average). Overconfident on Q14, Q16, Q6, Q12 (few called them hard, many lost the points); slightly underconfident on Q15, Q10.
  • Hard perceived vs actual: the questions marked hard were the ones fewer got right (r = -0.52 across Q4–Q24).
  • Expected vs earned: students expected less than they got: the largest group (43%) expected 50–69 points, while 68% scored 70 or more.
  • Problem sets vs midterm: r = 0.49 (n = 77), driven mostly by doing the sets at all (sets submitted r = 0.48; average of the submitted sets only r = 0.25).
  • Attendance vs midterm: r = 0.13 (n = 77), almost no link.

The common mistakes

Questions where one wrong option drew at least 10% of the class:

  • Q16 (53% chose b), right answer a): Put each recipe number on its own good: min{2x, 7y}. A burger takes 2 bun halves and 7 pickle slices, so the number of burgers is min{x/2, y/7}; multiplying by 14 gives min{7x, 2y}.
  • Q6 (39% chose a), right answer b): Left out III: with demand shifting right and supply unchanged, the price rises. Perhaps "shops pay the same costs" was read as "the price stays the same".
  • Q17 (25% chose a), right answer e): Kept €36 on the second segment. The first 3 drinks are free, so after them the student still has the whole €36: 4(x − 3) + y = 36, i.e. 4x + y = 48 for 3 ≤ x ≤ 9.
  • Q14 (23% chose b), right answer c): Missed that v = 6xy + 7 is an increasing transformation of u = xy: it ranks bundles the same way (I) and has the same MRS (II), but a different MUx.
  • Q12 (22% chose d), right answer a): Thought the circle drawing also breaks transitivity. Distance-based circles still rank every pair consistently; only "more is better" fails, because beyond (6, 7) more is worse.
  • Q21 (17% chose e), right answer b): Put too-large constants on the later segments (78 and 130 instead of 60 and 80); another 14% kept 54 on every segment.
  • Q24 (16% chose c), right answer d): Chose the kink (10, 5) instead of the corner (16, 0).
  • Q19 (14% chose b), right answer d): Chose one corner only. With these prices both corners give the same utility, so both are optimal.
  • Q23 (14% chose e), right answer b): Chose (3, 8), a point on the first segment, instead of the kink (6, 7).
  • Q5 (13% chose b), right answer c): Treated price-taking as violated because there are only two makers. The article says no producer or buyer can move the world price, so II holds; only "many small sellers" (I) and free entry (V) fail.
  • Q11 (13% chose d), right answer c): Ruled out Gen Z I, whose revealed preferences are in fact rational, and kept only III.
  • Q13 (13% chose d), right answer b): Spotted that the thick indifference area violates "more is better" (IV) but did not match the graphs to the two kinds of choices (II and V).
  • Q22 (12% chose b), right answer a): Chose the kink (6, 7) instead of the corner (0, 9).
  • Q20 (10% chose d), right answer c): Found one of the two optimal bundles, (3, 12), but paired it with (4, 8) instead of (6, 6).
