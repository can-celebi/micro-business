Midterm 19.11: lecturer report

In short

  • Calibration: on average a 10-point gap between the share who called a question hard and the share who got it wrong (11 weighted by points). Students saw the exam as somewhat easier than their results (-8 on average). Overconfident on Q14, Q16, Q6, Q12 (few called them hard, many lost the points); slightly underconfident on Q15, Q10.
  • Hard perceived vs actual: the questions marked hard were the ones fewer got right (r = -0.64 across the 24 questions).
  • Expected vs earned: students expected less than they got: the largest group (43%) expected 50–69 points, while 68% scored 70 or more.
  • Problem sets vs midterm: r = 0.49 (n = 77), driven mostly by doing the sets at all (sets submitted r = 0.48; average of the submitted sets only r = 0.25).
  • Attendance vs midterm: r = 0.13 (n = 77), almost no link.

1 · Calibration

Each dot is one question. On the dashed diagonal, the share who called it hard equals the share who got it wrong. Below the diagonal: students were overconfident (fewer called it hard than got it wrong); above: underconfident.

Calibration: share who called a question hard vs share who got it wrong

ECE-style measure = mean |share marked hard − share wrong| over the 24 questions = 0.105 (0.112 weighted by points). Marking a question hard is a yes/no judgement, not a probability, so this is calibration in a loose sense.

5 · Problem sets vs midterm

Problem sets vs midterm (groups of students)

r = 0.49, n = 77 (average of PS1–PS4 with a missing set = 0). Averaging only the submitted sets gives r = 0.25; the number of sets submitted gives r = 0.48. So most of the link is whether students did the sets at all. Dots are groups of students (bigger dot = more students); students with the same average are always in the same group.

6 · Attendance vs midterm

Attendance vs midterm (groups of students)

r = 0.13, n = 77 (lectures attended L02–L10, from signed-in taps). 21 students are at 0 and probably never signed in; without them r = 0.16 (n = 56). Almost no link either way.
