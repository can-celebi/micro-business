completeness

For any two lotteries: you prefer one of them, or they are exactly as good.
violation
a raffle for a trip or a raffle for a laptop?
'I can't say which, and I don't find them equally good either.'
Wieland 451f6da6e050 K1.

transitivity

If 1 is at least as good as 2, and 2 at least as good as 3, then 1 is at least as good as 3.
violation
pizza over pasta, pasta over salad, salad over pizza
a circle
Wieland 451f6da6e050 K2.

continuity

If S ≻ H ≻ C, some chance of S (C otherwise), between 0% and 100%, is exactly as good as H.
violation
cake ≻ bread ≻ a night in hospital, and you are allergic to nuts
'I take the bread, however tiny the chance that the cake contains a nut.'
then
the ticket 'cake with p, hospital otherwise' is never exactly as good as the bread: worse for every p below 100%, better at 100%

Expected utility (EU): multiply the utility of each outcome by its probability and add up.
the long way
EU = p₁ · u(x₁) + p₂ · u(x₂) + … + pₙ · u(xₙ)
short
EU = Σ pᵢ · u(xᵢ)
Σ
a capital Greek S, for 'sum': add up over all outcomes
read it
for every outcome, multiply its probability by its utility; then add everything up
ticket A
0.3 · 1 + 0.7 · 0.6: two outcomes, two terms
also
EV = Σ pᵢ · xᵢ
