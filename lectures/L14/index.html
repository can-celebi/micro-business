probabilities

A probability is a number between 0 and 1 that says how likely an outcome is; the probabilities of all possible outcomes add up to 1.
weather · rain tomorrow with probability 1/5, so dry with 1 − 1/5 = 4/5
add up to 1 · 1/5 + 4/5 = 1: one of the two must happen
two coins · each toss ½; the two tosses do not depend on each other
multiply · HH, HT, TH, TT: each ½ · ½ = ¼
at least one head · HH, HT, TH: ¼ + ¼ + ¼ = 3/4
Wieland's L14 §1. Three moves only: read a probability off a story, get the missing one as 1 minus the others, multiply for two things that do not depend on each other. The tree builds with the clicks: first toss, second toss, then the four ¼'s.

expected value

The expected value (EV) of a gamble: multiply each outcome by its probability and add up.
a coin game · heads you win €30, tails you lose €10
EV · ½ · 30 + ½ · (−10) = 15 − 5 = €10
the meaning · the average result per game if you played it many, many times
on the line · EV is the balance point of the two outcomes
Wieland's L14 §2.

a ranking is not enough

So far, utility only ranked things: any numbers in the right order worked.
three prizes
a phone, headphones, a coffee
the ranking
phone, then headphones, then coffee
an offer
give back the headphones for a ticket: phone 50%, coffee 50%
numbers (10, 3, 0)
the ticket gives 5 on average, more than 3: take the ticket
numbers (10, 8, 0)
the ticket still gives 5, less than 8: keep the headphones
so
same ranking, opposite answers

the measuring question

Measure each prize with one question.
the worst
u(coffee) = 0
the best
u(phone) = 1
the question
which chance p of the phone (coffee otherwise) is exactly as good as the headphones for sure?
our student
p = 60%
so
u(headphones) = 0.6

try it: measure a prize

Lena ranks three prizes: a concert ticket (best), a cinema ticket, nothing (worst). We set u(concert) = 1 and u(nothing) = 0. Lena is exactly indifferent between the cinema ticket for sure and a lottery that gives the concert ticket with probability 2/5 and nothing with probability 3/5.
What is u(cinema)?
answer
2/5
at indifference u(cinema) = 2/5 · u(concert) + 3/5 · u(nothing)
= 2/5 · 1 + 3/5 · 0 = 2/5

every ticket is a chance of the best prize

Swap each middle prize for its equally good ticket.
ticket A · phone 30%, headphones 70%
headphones · = phone 60%, coffee 40%
ticket A · phone 30% + 70% × 60% = 72%
ticket B · phone 75%, coffee 25%
so · B gives the phone more often: B is better
in utility · EU(A) = 0.72 < EU(B) = 0.75
Wieland's step 3: expected utility = the chance of the best prize. The swap is the independence axiom (next slide).

properties of preferences (the vNM axioms)

With these four properties, a person chooses between lotteries as if maximising expected utility.
complete
any two lotteries can be compared
transitive
A ≿ B and B ≿ C, so A ≿ C
continuity
a middle prize equals some chance of the best prize: the measuring question
independence
mixing both tickets with the same third ticket keeps their order: the swap
Can can say: next week Allais and Ellsberg attack independence. Von Neumann and Morgenstern, 1944. Wieland: one plain sentence each. Say it is not Arrow's independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA) from voting. Source line (Arif): von Neumann and Morgenstern, Theory of Games and Economic Behavior, 1944; proof in the 2nd ed., 1947.

properties of vNM utility

What the numbers mean, and what you may change.
measured
u(prize) = the chance of the best prize that is just as good
the rule
a gamble is better when its expected utility is higher
rescale
a + b·u with b > 0 gives exactly the same choices
like temperature
°F = 32 + 1.8 · °C: other numbers, the same weather
not allowed
u²: headphones drop to 0.36, and the 50/50 ticket (0.5) now wins
differences matter
(0, 0.6, 1) and 3 + 2·u = (3, 4.2, 5): gaps 0.6 : 0.4 and 1.2 : 0.8, both 3 : 2
cardinal
not only the order, also the size of the steps means something

expected utility

Expected utility (EU): multiply the utility of each outcome by its probability and add up.
EV
multiply each outcome in euros by its probability, add up
EU
the same, with U(M), the utility of each outcome
the symbol
EU = Σ p · U(M)
Σ
the Greek letter sigma: "add up over all outcomes"
the rule
take the gamble if its EU is above U(wealth without it)
Can, Addition 12: the idea in words first; explain the Σ symbol in plain words. Say 'multiply by its probability and add up' (Can). M is total wealth (Frank). The numbers come after the shapes.

linear, concave, convex

Three shapes a utility function can have.
linear · a straight line: U = M
concave · bends down, flatter and flatter: U = √M
convex · bends up, steeper and steeper: U = M²
Can, Addition 12: the shapes before the numbers.

marginal utility on the curve

Marginal utility: how much U rises (Δu) when wealth rises by one step (Δx).
U = M
constant marginal utility
U = √M
diminishing: each euro adds less
U = M²
increasing: each euro adds more
Δx = +1 atU = MU = √MU = M²
M = 1 → 2+1+0.41+3
M = 4 → 5+1+0.24+9
M = 9 → 10+1+0.16+19
Can, Addition 12: Δx → Δu at three points for each type. √2 − 1 = 0.414, √5 − 2 = 0.236, √10 − 3 = 0.162 (rounded to 2 decimals); 2² − 1² = 3, 5² − 4² = 9, 10² − 9² = 19.

expected utility with numbers

Wealth €400, U(M) = √M. A coin bet: win €129 or lose €39.
wealth after · €529 or €361
utilities · √529 = 23 and √361 = 19
EU · ½ · 23 + ½ · 19 = 21
without the bet · √400 = 20; 21 > 20, so the person accepts
a common slip · ½ · 529 + ½ · 361 = 445 is expected wealth, not expected utility

the shape of U and risk

With money, the shape of U says how a person treats risk.
concave · diminishing marginal utility: risk averse
linear · constant marginal utility: risk neutral
convex · increasing marginal utility: risk seeking
why · with concave U, the euros you could lose weigh more than the euros you could win
Wieland §5 (the link shape ↔ attitude); comes after EU with numbers (Can's order, Addition 12).

expected pot · 4 boxes €8 · 6 boxes €9 · 8 boxes €8
fewer than 6 · risk averse: U = √M opens 4
exactly 6 · risk neutral: U = M
more than 6 · risk seeking: U = M² opens 8
