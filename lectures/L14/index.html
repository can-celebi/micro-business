probabilities

A probability is a number between 0 and 1 that says how likely an outcome is; the probabilities of all possible outcomes add up to 1.
weather · rain tomorrow with probability 1/5, so dry with 1 − 1/5 = 4/5
add up to 1 · 1/5 + 4/5 = 1: one of the two must happen
two coins · each toss ½; the two tosses do not depend on each other
multiply · HH, HT, TH, TT: each ½ · ½ = ¼
at least one head · HH, HT, TH: ¼ + ¼ + ¼ = 3/4
Wieland's L14 §1. Three moves only: read a probability off a story, get the missing one as 1 minus the others, multiply for two things that do not depend on each other. The tree builds with the clicks: first toss, second toss, then the four ¼'s.

expected value

The expected value (EV) of a gamble: multiply each outcome by its probability and add up.
a coin game · heads you win €30, tails you lose €10
EV · ½ · 30 + ½ · (−10) = 15 − 5 = €10
the meaning · the average result per game if you played it many, many times
on the line · EV is the balance point of the two outcomes
Wieland's L14 §2.

try it: measure a prize

Lena ranks three prizes: a concert ticket (best), a cinema ticket, nothing (worst). We set u(concert) = 1 and u(nothing) = 0. Lena is exactly indifferent between the cinema ticket for sure and a lottery that gives the concert ticket with probability 2/5 and nothing with probability 3/5.

answer
2/5

what we assumed

Four properties of preferences over tickets (the vNM axioms).
complete
any two tickets can be compared
transitive
the ranking does not go round in a circle
continuity
when you turn the chance, it stops somewhere: exactly as good
independence
mixing two tickets with the same third ticket keeps their order: the swap
Wieland step 8. Two from L06, two new. Not Arrow's IIA. Can can say: next week Allais and Ellsberg attack independence. Von Neumann and Morgenstern, 1944 (2nd ed. 1947).

properties of vNM utility

Student 1's numbers: coffee 0, headphones 0.6, phone 1.
the number · 0.6: as good as a 60% chance of the phone
it ranks gambles · more utility, multiplied by the probabilities and added up, is better
allowed · v = a + b·u with b > 0
example · v = 10 + 20u: coffee 10, headphones 22, phone 30
same answer · headphones vs the 50–50 ticket: 0.6 > 0.5, and 22 > 20
why b > 0 · v = 1 − u turns the best prize into the worst
Wieland §3b, one property per click. Hands (yes/no): does 10 + 20u keep the ranking of every gamble? (yes) · is 1 − u allowed? (no). Like Celsius and Fahrenheit.

expected utility

Expected utility (EU): multiply the utility of each outcome by its probability and add up.
the long way
EU = p₁ · u(x₁) + p₂ · u(x₂) + … + pₙ · u(xₙ)
short
EU = Σ pᵢ · u(xᵢ)
Σ
a capital Greek S, for 'sum': add up over all outcomes
read it
for every outcome, multiply its probability by its utility; then add everything up
ticket A
0.3 · 1 + 0.7 · 0.6: two outcomes, two terms
also
EV = Σ pᵢ · xᵢ
Wieland §4: the long sum first, then Σ, read aloud. Point at each term of ticket A. No money yet.

concave, linear, convex

Join two points of the curve with a straight line.
concave · the line lies below the curve: U(M) = √M
linear · the line is the curve: U(M) = 2M
convex · the line lies above the curve: U(M) = M²
all three · go up: more wealth is better
Wieland §4b: draw the curve, two points, the line; ask 'below or above?'. Risk attitudes come later (5a).

marginal utility on the curve: concave

Equal steps Δx in wealth; read off how much utility each adds, Δu.
4 → 100 · Δu = 10 − 2 = 8
100 → 196 · Δu = 14 − 10 = 4
529 → 625 · Δu = 25 − 23 = 2
concave · the same €96 adds less and less: diminishing MU
Wieland §5: Δx first, then Δu, one per click.

marginal utility on the curve: linear

Equal steps Δx in wealth; read off how much utility each adds, Δu.
0 → 2 · Δu = 4 − 0 = 4
4 → 6 · Δu = 12 − 8 = 4
8 → 10 · Δu = 20 − 16 = 4
linear · always 4: constant MU
Wieland §5.

marginal utility on the curve: convex

Equal steps Δx in wealth; read off how much utility each adds, Δu.
0 → 2 · Δu = 4 − 0 = 4
4 → 6 · Δu = 36 − 16 = 20
8 → 10 · Δu = 100 − 64 = 36
convex · more and more: increasing MU
Wieland §5. Differences in utility mean something only because vNM utility is cardinal (3b).
