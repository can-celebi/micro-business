explicit vs implicit cost

explicit
money leaves your pocket · a payment, a bill
implicit
no money changes hands, but you give up something valuable · your time, your effort, what your own resources could earn
economic cost
explicit + implicit
Implicit = mostly the time and effort you put in, or what your own money/equipment could have earned elsewhere.

sunk cost

already paid, can't get back → ignore it
A cost already paid that you cannot get back, whatever you decide now, so it should not affect the decision.
you
a non-refundable concert ticket · you're sick that night
openai
a training run that disappoints · spent is spent
a company
a half-built factory nobody needs any more
your gpu
paid €2,000 · resale €1,200 → only €800 is sunk
sunk part
no opportunity cost · nothing left to give up
resellable part
not sunk · keeping the GPU costs you the €1,200 you could get
Only what you cannot get back is sunk. A GPU you can resell is only partly sunk: the resale value is still yours, so keeping the GPU has an opportunity cost (the €1,200). Only future costs and benefits matter for decisions.

how averages move

If your new grade is higher than your current average, the average goes up; if it is lower, it goes down.
examgradeyour averagewhy
19090first exam
2708070 is lower than 90 → down
3958595 is higher than 80 → up
48585same as the average → no change
your friends
a very tall friend joins → the group's average height goes up
your subscriptions
you add a free app → the average price per subscription goes down
why care?
firms work the same way · if one more unit costs less than the average, the average cost falls
Averages after each exam: 90; (90+70)/2 = 80; (90+70+95)/3 = 85; (90+70+95+85)/4 = 85. The new grade is the "marginal" value: it moves the average toward itself. PS1 Q3 (falling course average) uses exactly this.

positive vs normative

what is · what should be
A positive statement says what is and can be checked with facts; a normative statement says what should be.
positive
can be checked with data
normative
a value judgement: "should", "too high", "fair"
Economists try to keep the two apart: first what is, then what should be.
