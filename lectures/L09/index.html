one example for the whole lecture

u=x⁢y
prices
px=3, py=1, money B=60 (as at the end of last lecture)
three bundles
all on the budget line: A=(5,45), C=(15,15), E=(10,30)
question
which one is the best feasible bundle, and how can we tell without drawing?
Check: 3·5 + 45 = 60, 3·15 + 15 = 60, 3·10 + 30 = 60. E is L08's best feasible bundle.

way 1 · the picture

at A
the indifference curve through A is steeper than the budget line: moving toward more x reaches better curves
at C
A and C are equally good (both u = 225); at C the curve is flatter than the line: move back toward more y
at E
the curve u = 300 just touches the line: the same slope, the best feasible bundle
A: 5·45 = 225, C: 15·15 = 225, E: 10·30 = 300. Steep/flat = MRS above/below the price ratio 3.

marginal utility

name
marginal utility, MUx and MUy
meaning
how much u rises with one more unit of a good, the other good held fixed (a derivative)
here
for u=x⁢y: MUx=y and MUy=x. Check at A: from (5, 45) to (6, 45), u goes from 225 to 270: +45 = y
u = xy is linear in x for fixed y, so the one-unit step gives MU_x = y exactly.

way 2 · bang for the buck

Utility per euro spent on each good. If one good gives more per euro, move money to it.
bundleMUxpxMUypya euro moved …
A (5, 45)453=1551=5from y to x gains
C (15, 15)153=5151=15from x to y gains
E (10, 30)303=10101=10gains nothing
At the best feasible bundle, the last euro does equally well on both goods. Remember this number, 10: it comes back.
Equal bang for the buck: MU_x/p_x = MU_y/p_y, the same as MRS = MU_x/MU_y = p_x/p_y.

way 3 · arrows

along the line
one more x and three less y cost the same: the step d=(1,−3) stays on the budget line, and so does any multiple of it, big or small
the prices
the price vector p=(3,1) is perpendicular to the line: p⋅d=3⋅1+1⋅(−3)=0
better
the arrow of marginal utilities (MUx,MUy)=(30,10) at E points across the curve, toward better bundles
Picture first, names after (R42). Arrows scaled to fit: d drawn as 5·(1, −3), p as 4·(3, 1), ∇u as 0.8·(30, 10).

the gradient

name
the gradient, ∇u=(MUx,MUy)
meaning
an arrow made of the two marginal utilities
what it shows
the direction in which utility rises fastest; it is perpendicular to the indifference curve

project the gradient onto the budget line

How much of the gradient points along the line? Multiply it with the step d = (1, −3).
bundle∇u∇u⋅dalong the line
A (5, 45)(45, 5)45 − 15 = 30toward more x is better
C (15, 15)(15, 15)15 − 45 = −30toward more y is better
E (10, 30)(30, 10)30 − 30 = 0no step helps: the best feasible bundle
At E the gradient has nothing left along the line: it is perpendicular to the line, like the price vector. So it is a multiple of it: ∇u=λ⁢p, here (30,10)=10⋅(3,1).
Can's projection: the length of the projection is (∇u·d)/|d|; the sign and the zero are what matter. 10 is the bang for the buck from way 2.

three pictures, one condition

∇u⋅d=0
no step along the line helps
so
MUx⁢py−MUy⁢px=0
same as
MUxMUy=pxpy: MRS = price ratio (way 1)
same as
MUxpx=MUypy: equal bang for the buck (way 2)

way 4 · the Lagrangian

ℒ=x⁢y+λ⁢(60−3x−y)
name
the Lagrangian, with a new number λ (lambda)
meaning
utility, plus λ times the money left over; on the budget line the money left over is 0
what it does
its derivatives, set to zero, give the arrow condition of way 3 and the budget line at once

the first-order conditions

∂ℒ∂x=y−3λ=0∂ℒ∂y=x−λ=0∂ℒ∂λ=60−3x−y=0
The first two say (y,x)=λ⁢(3,1): the gradient is a multiple of the price vector (way 3). The third is the budget line.

solve

from 1 and 2
x=λ and y=3λ, so y=3x (MRS yx = 3 = price ratio)
into 3
3x+3x=60, so x=10, y=30
and
λ=10: the bang for the buck at E (way 2) and the multiple in way 3

what λ measures

name
λ, the Lagrange multiplier
meaning
the number with ∇u=λ⁢p at the best feasible bundle
what it measures
how much utility one more euro of money buys, at the best feasible bundle

Best bundle with B: x = B/6, y = B/2, u* = B²/12. Shown as smaller and smaller steps, no approximation formula (R41).

exercise 1 · climbing and cinema

Each week you spend €72 on climbing-gym visits (x, €6 each) and cinema tickets (y, €12 each). Your preferences:
u=x1/3⁢y2/3
budget line
6x+12y=72, ends (12, 0) and (0, 6)
your turn
find the best feasible bundle; then check it with bang for the buck and with the Lagrangian
Leo E1, numbers checked by Sinan and Murat. Not a PS4 or midterm form (exponents 1/3, 2/3).

exercise 1 · three ways

MRS
MUx=13x−2/3y2/3, MUy=23x1/3y−1/3, so MRS=y2x
= price ratio
y2x=612, so y=x; budget: 18x=72, the bundle (4, 4), u*=4
bang for the buck
at (4, 4): MUxpx=1/36=118 and MUypy=2/312=118: equal
Lagrangian
the first two conditions divided give the same y2x=12, and λ=118
Leo E1 steps 2–4. Spending shares 24/72 = 1/3 and 48/72 = 2/3: the exponents.

exercise 1 · what λ means

any budget
the best feasible bundle is x=y=B18, so u*=B18
one more euro
the budget line shifts outwards to B = 73: u* goes from 4 to 7318, up by exactly 118=λ
why exact
here u* grows in a straight line with the money, so every extra euro adds the same λ
Leo E1 step 5. Contrast with the running example, where u* = B²/12 grows faster and the ratio only settles at λ.

exercise 2 · gel pens and pencils

For your notes, 2 gel pens are exactly as good as 3 pencils. Budget €12, a pencil costs €1. (x = gel pens, y = pencils)
u=3x+2y
gel pen €2
a gel pen is worth 3/2 pencils to you (MRS 3/2) but costs 2: pencils win every euro (3/2 per euro against 2). The corner: 0 gel pens, 12 pencils
so
MRS = price ratio cannot hold here, and that is fine
gel pen €1.20

picture
the budget line turns flatter than the indifference lines, and the best point jumps from one end to the other

exercise 3 · toy tricycles

Each tricycle needs 1 frame and 3 wheels. A frame costs €30, a wheel €10, budget €180. (x = frames, y = wheels)
u=min⁡{3x,y}
one tricycle
costs 30 + 3 · 10 = €60, so €180 buys 3: the bundle (3, 9)
no tangency
the indifference curve has a kink on the ray y = 3x: no single slope there, so "MRS = price ratio" cannot be used
wasted money
a 4th frame without 3 more wheels adds no tricycle
Leo E3. Budget line from (6, 0) to (0, 18); 30·3 + 10·9 = 180.

when the rule does not apply

the rule
MRS = price ratio finds a best feasible bundle where the indifference curve touches the line inside, away from the ends
a corner
if moving along the line always helps in one direction, you keep going until the line ends: all money on one good. There the gradient is not a multiple of the price vector, yet no feasible step helps
a kink
if the curve has a corner, its slope (the MRS) is not defined there, so the rule cannot even be written down
Raif: say once, in words and with a picture, why the conditions fail at a corner, so the cases do not look like the method breaking.

corners · perfect substitutes

u=2x+3y
MRS = 2/3 at every bundle; money 12, py=3
p_x = 1
price ratio 1/3 < MRS 2/3: each euro does more on x (2 per euro against 1). All on x: the corner (12, 0)
p_x = 3
price ratio 1 > 2/3: all on y: the corner (0, 4)
p_x = 2
price ratio 2/3 = MRS: the indifference line lies on the budget line, every point on it is best
arrows
∇u=(2,3) is a multiple of p=(px,3) only when px=2: otherwise no interior solution, and the sign of ∇u⋅d (+3 or −3) says which end
Fatih 4(i), final (Murat, Kamuran, Raif): not midterm A13/B13, not Leo E2. d = (3, −p_x).

a kink · perfect complements

u=min⁡{x,y}
sausages and buns, 1 : 1; prices 1 and 3, money 20
buy in the ratio
x=y: anything unmatched is wasted money
on the line
x+3x=20, so the bundle (5, 5)
why the rule fails
at the kink the MRS is not defined, so "MRS = price ratio" cannot be used; the ratio rule replaces it
Fatih 4(ii). Budget x + 3y = 20; L-curves min = 3, 5, 7.

interior or corner · quasi-linear

u=2x+y
the mixture from L08; prices 8 and 1
MRS
MUx=2, MUy=12y, so MRS=4y
money 20
4y=8 gives y=4, then x=20−48=2: the bundle (2, 4)
money 2
not even enough for y=4: all on y, the corner (0, 2)
insight
once y=4 is reached, extra money goes only into x
Fatih 4(iii). Curves: u = 6 through (2, 4); u = √2 through (0, 2). At (0, 2) the MRS is 4√2, below 8.
